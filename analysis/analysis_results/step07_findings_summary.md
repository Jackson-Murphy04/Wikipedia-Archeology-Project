# Findings summary

## Dataset scale

313,269 revision records cover 31 articles; median 9,034 records per article.

## Time coverage

Valid timestamps span 2001-05-14 08:28:10+00:00 through 2026-11-10 06:27:03+00:00 (25.5 years).

## Article growth

The largest net observed byte increase is September 11 attacks (+323,030 bytes); byte size is a proxy, not a measure of historical completeness.

## Contributor concentration

Median top-10 username revision share across articles is 17.4%. Usernames and metadata do not establish identity or expertise.

## Event-category comparison

Category medians describe the articles assigned to each primary category; category sample sizes range from 2 to 10.

## Editing spikes

Per-article robust monthly outliers are listed in step02_article_monthly_spikes.csv. A spike is an observed anomaly, not evidence of its cause.

## Content changes

The retrieved sample documents vandalism/reversion, maintenance-template edits, a bibliography addition, and a list-item removal; it is too small to characterize all content changes or verify historical claims.

## Limitations

Revision metadata does not encode all article text, explain editing motivation, or establish contributor identity. Contextual claims need independent dated sources. Categories are researcher-assigned.
